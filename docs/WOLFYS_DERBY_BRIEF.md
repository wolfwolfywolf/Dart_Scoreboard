## Copy blocks

**Meta description / one-liner (122 chars)**

> Wolfy's Derby is a horse racing dice game for 2–6 players. Draw a horse, ante up,
> and roll — first one home takes the pot.

**Short blurb (card, grid tile, ~40 words)**

> Six horses line up. You draw one at random, everyone antes a chip, and the dice do
> the rest. Every color that comes up moves that horse a space. First across the line
> takes the whole pot.

**Medium (product page intro, ~95 words)**

> Wolfy's Derby is a horse racing game you can teach in about thirty seconds. Each
> player draws a horse at random and antes a chip into the pot. On your turn you roll
> three dice — but these dice don't show numbers, they show horses. Every color that
> comes up gallops one space closer to the finish, no matter who rolled. Because three
> dice land at once, two horses can cross together for a photo finish and split the
> pot. It plays just as well with a five-year-old as with a table of adults.

**Long (about page / full description)**

> Wolfy's Derby strips horse racing down to the part everyone actually likes: watching
> your horse run and having no control over it whatsoever.
>
> Six horses, one per lane, each a different color. You don't choose your horse — you
> roll for it, which means you're just as likely to end up on the one that comes last.
> Everyone antes a chip into the middle, and from there the dice run the race.
>
> The three dice are the trick. Instead of pips they carry the six horse colors, so a
> roll of red-gold-gold moves the red horse one space and the gold horse two. It
> doesn't matter who rolled — every horse named by a die moves. There are no decisions
> to make, which is exactly why a young child can play it on equal terms with an adult.
>
> With fewer than six players some horses go undrawn, and they still run. If one of
> those wins, nobody collects: the pot stays, everyone antes again, and the next race
> is worth double, which gets loud fast.

## How it plays (for a "how to play" section)

1. Stand each jockey on its matching colored tile at the starting line.
2. Take turns rolling one die — the color you roll is your horse. If it's taken, roll
   again. Everyone antes one chip into the pot.
3. On your turn roll all three dice. Each die moves the horse of that color one space.
4. First horse across the FINISH line wins, and whoever drew it takes the pot.
5. If two horses cross on the same roll, they split it.

House rules worth mentioning: **Full Field** (2–3 players draw two horses each),
**High Stakes** (ante two chips), **Triple Crown** (three races, biggest stack wins),
**Long Odds** (name a second horse; if it wins, take half the pot).

*2026-10-17: the rules sheet was revised. "Riderless horses" became "Horses Nobody
Drew" (never say riderless), photo finishes can be three-way, an undrawn winner means
everyone antes again for a double pot, and "If You Are Teaching" was added. The
rules sheet image on the site is the source of truth where this brief differs.*

## Components

- Bi-fold race board, 18 × 9 in, twelve spaces per lane
- 3 custom color dice, 16 mm — one face per horse
- 6 jockey pawns in matching racing silks
- Illustrated rules sheet
- *Chips are not included* — the game is played with coins, buttons, or candy

## Brand

**Palette** — the six lane colors are the backbone of the whole identity. They appear
on the board, the dice, and the jockeys, and should carry through to the site.

| Lane | Hex | Silks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `#a8341f` red | diagonal sash |
| 2 | `#2f6ea5` blue | chest band |
| 3 | `#c1902a` gold | chevron |
| 4 | `#c4621a` orange | hoops |
| 5 | `#6d4a91` purple | cross |
| 6 | `#37773f` green | yoke |

Supporting colors: felt green `#4b6636`, cell green `#6d884b`, wood `#4a2a11` →
`#a8723c`, cream lettering `#f3e7c6`, gold rule `#e2c690`.

**Type** — a classic serif set in small caps with generous letter-spacing, cream on
dark green, with a soft dark emboss. Think old painted pub sign or vintage racetrack
board, not modern geometric sans.

**Visual direction** — warm wood frame, mottled green felt field, brass hardware,
checkered flags, a brass horseshoe. Everything is drawn flat and bold rather than
photographic.

**Accessibility note** — never rely on color alone. Each horse has a number *and* a
distinct silks pattern for exactly this reason; carry that habit into the site.

## Status — read before writing any page

The artwork is finished and production files are prepared for The Game Crafter, but
**nothing has been manufactured or test-printed yet.** As of this writing there is no
price, no stock, and no purchase link.

So the site should not say the game is for sale or available. Treat it as *in
development* or *coming soon*, and keep a single clear call to action (an email signup
for when it's ready) rather than a buy button.

**Two details are still placeholders** and should not be presented as final:
- "Ages 5+" — chosen to fit the layout, not from any testing
- "2–6 Players" — correct by design, but never playtested at the extremes

**One legal caution:** "derby" on its own is a generic word and fine to use. Avoid
anything implying a connection to the Kentucky Derby — Churchill Downs enforces that
mark aggressively, including the roses-and-juleps visual cues. The current green and
brass direction is well clear of it; keep it that way.
