A Horse Racing Dice Game

Wolfy's Derby

Roll the Dice · Match the Colors · Gallop to the Finish

Six horses line up. You draw one at random, everyone antes a chip, and the dice do the rest. Every color that comes up moves that horse a space. First across the line takes the whole pot.

Wolfy's Derby is a horse racing game you can teach in about thirty seconds. Each player draws a horse at random and antes a chip into the pot. On your turn you roll three dice, but these dice don't show numbers, they show horses. Every color that comes up gallops one space closer to the finish, no matter who rolled. Because three dice land at once, two horses can cross together for a photo finish and split the pot. It plays just as well with a five-year-old as with a table of adults.

How to play

  1. Stand each jockey on its matching colored tile at the starting line.
  2. Take turns rolling one die. The color you roll is your horse; if it's taken, roll again. Everyone antes one chip into the pot.
  3. On your turn roll all three dice. Each die moves the horse of that color one space.
  4. First horse across the FINISH line wins, and whoever drew it takes the pot.
  5. If two horses cross on the same roll, they split it.

House rules

  • Full Field: with 2 or 3 players, everyone draws two horses.
  • High Stakes: ante two chips.
  • Triple Crown: best of three races.

With fewer than six players some horses run riderless. If one of those wins, nobody collects and the whole pot rolls over into the next race.

In the box

  • Bi-fold race board, 18 × 9 in, twelve spaces per lane
  • 3 custom color dice, 16 mm, one face per horse
  • 6 jockey pawns in matching racing silks
  • Illustrated rules sheet

Chips are not included. Play with coins, buttons or candy.

Why it works

Wolfy's Derby strips horse racing down to the part everyone actually likes: watching your horse run and having no control over it whatsoever.

You don't choose your horse; you roll for it, which means you're just as likely to end up on the one that comes last. Everyone antes a chip into the middle, and from there the dice run the race. There are no decisions to make, which is exactly why a young child can play it on equal terms with an adult.

And when a riderless horse wins, the whole pot rolls over into the next race, which gets loud fast.
