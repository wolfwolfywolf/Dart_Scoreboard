Wolfy's Derby is a horse racing game you can teach in about thirty seconds. Each player draws a horse at random and antes a chip into the pot. On your turn you roll three dice, but these dice don't show pips, they show horses. Every color that comes up gallops one space closer to the finish, no matter who rolled. Because three dice land at once, two horses can cross together for a photo finish and split the pot. It plays just as well with a five-year-old as with a table of adults.

How to play

  1. Stand each jockey on its matching colored tile at the starting line.
  2. Take turns rolling one die. The color you roll is your horse; if it's taken, roll again. Everyone puts one chip in the middle: that's the pot.
  3. The youngest player goes first and play passes to the left. On your turn roll all three dice. Each die moves the horse of that color one space, so a roll of red, red, red gallops red three spaces.
  4. It doesn't matter who rolled. Every color shown moves, even horses nobody drew.
  5. The first horse across the FINISH line wins, and whoever drew it takes the whole pot.

Photo finish

Two or even three horses can cross on the same roll. They all win and split the pot evenly; any leftover chips stay for the next race.

Horses nobody drew

With fewer than six players some horses go undrawn, and they still run. If one of them wins, nobody collects: the pot stays on the table, everyone antes again, and the next race is worth double.

House rules

  • Full Field: with 2 or 3 players, everyone draws two horses and antes a chip for each, so every lane has an owner.
  • High Stakes: ante two chips instead of one.
  • Triple Crown: three races; the biggest stack takes the trophy.
  • Long Odds: before the race each player may name a second horse. If it wins, they take half the pot.

If you are teaching

Young players don't need the numbers at all. Say the colors out loud ("red, gold, gold") and let them move the horses. The counting takes care of itself.

Why it works

Wolfy's Derby strips horse racing down to the part everyone actually likes: watching your horse run and having no control over it whatsoever.

You don't choose your horse; you roll for it, which means you're just as likely to end up on the one that comes last. Everyone antes a chip into the middle, and from there the dice run the race. There are no decisions to make, which is exactly why a young child can play it on equal terms with an adult.

And when a horse nobody drew wins, the pot stays put, everyone antes again, and the next race is worth double, which gets loud fast.
